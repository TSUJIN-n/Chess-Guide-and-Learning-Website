Checkmate

Checkmate is the end of the game. It happens when your opponent's king is in check and has no legal moves to escape. When this happens, the game is over, and you win!

THE BASICS

What is Checkmate?

Checkmate happens when the king is in check and there are no legal moves for the king to escape. It means your opponent cannot stop the attack, and the game ends immediately.

Example: Fastest Checkmate

1

White moves the pawn from e2 to e4 to control the center, while Black moves the pawn from e7 to e5 to contest the center.

2

White moves the queen from d1 to h5 to prepare an attack on the f7 pawn, while Black moves the knight from b8 to c6 to develop a piece.

3

White moves the bishop from f1 to c4 to target the f7 pawn alongside the queen, while Black moves the knight from g8 to f6, failing to stop White's checkmate threat.

4

White moves the queen from h5 to f7, capturing the pawn and delivering checkmate. The Black king has no legal escape, so White wins!

Common Checkmate Patterns

Back Rank Mate

The king is trapped on the back rank.

Smothered Mate

The king is surrounded by its own pieces.

Ladder Mate

A series of checks with the queen.

Hook Mate

The king is trapped by a rook and pawn.

Key Takeaways

Checkmate happens when the king is in check and has no legal moves.

You win the game when you checkmate your opponent's king.

There are several common checkmate patterns.

Practice these patterns to improve your endgame skills.
